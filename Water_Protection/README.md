# Water Protection
Water has become a pressing problem in the last decades, with experts warning that human activities causing pollution and climate change are drastically changing the water cycles we depend on for life. Water is one of the University of Waterloo's most important resources, supporting building operations, research activities, cooling and heating infrastructure, laboratories, residence facilities, landscaping, and daily campus life. In 2025, the University used approximately 648,936 m³ of water, representing a significant demand on regional water resources. Moreover, the Region of Waterloo is currently facing a water capacity crisis which led to the halting of new development in the region in 2026.  We need to find ways to make water consumption more sustainable, to ensure food and water security and the continuation of life. 

Moreover, campus operations use a large amount of salt for winter road maintenance and water softening operations every year, which can drain into a nearby drinking water zone impacting the health of urban waterways. Previous attempts to reduce or replace salt use have faced operational and safety challenges, creating a need for solutions that balance public safety, infrastructure requirements, water quality protection, and sustainability. Campus infrastructure must also manage increasing stormwater volumes resulting from more frequent extreme rainfall events.

## Challenge
Your challenge is to propose a solution to improve the sustainability, resilience, or efficiency of water systems at the University of Waterloo.  Teams may focus on water conservation, stormwater management, water reuse, water quality protection, reducing the impacts of salt use, or improving water monitoring and infrastructure management. Solutions should be technically feasible, economically viable, and capable of delivering measurable environmental benefits. 

In addition to addressing water system performance, teams should consider how their solution contributes to climate action, climate adaptation, and thriving ecosystems. Climate action measures may include reducing the energy and emissions associated with water treatment, distribution, and management. Climate adaptation measures may focus on increasing resilience to more frequent extreme rainfall events, drought conditions, flooding, or other climate-related impacts that affect campus infrastructure and operations. Teams are also encouraged to consider how their solution can support thriving ecosystems by protecting and enhancing water quality, restoring natural hydrological processes, improving watershed health, reducing contaminants entering local waterways, and supporting biodiversity. 

Your proposal should balance environmental performance, operational requirements, safety, cost-effectiveness, and long-term resilience. Consider how your solution can help the university address regional water challenges while contributing to cleaner water, healthier ecosystems, reduced greenhouse gas emissions, and a more climate-resilient campus. 

## Potential Solutions
These aren’t the only solutions, but these are some of the ideas that you can take inspiration from. Feel free to mix and match any potential solutions if you feel that it is appropriate.

| Potential Solutions | Description | Resources |
|:---------|:------------|:----------|
| **Rainwater Harvesting Solutions on buildings** | Using barrels and containers |Roof catchment systems 
| **Recycling Graywater** | Treating lightly contaminated water (from sinks and showers) for other uses (watering plants, flush toilets, etc.).|Greywater filtration systems  
| **Adding/building storm gardens/ artificial wetlands** |Building stormwater gardens to manage storm water run-off. It also has the added benefit of improving biodiversity. |City of Waterloo Stormwater management plan. 
| **Add more permeable surfaces across campus** |Permeable pavement can help control water runoff. This also means reducing the amount of paved road surface on campus. |Permeable concrete and permeable pavers 
| **Improve water fixtures across campus** |Modifying and refitting plumbing across campus  |Low flow faucets/shower heads 
| **Instrumentation** |Environmental sensors are implemented on campus to track indicators of sustainability, consumption, and pollution. |Arduinos, other sensors, and data analysis. Examples: electrical conductivity, Air quality, Traffic.
