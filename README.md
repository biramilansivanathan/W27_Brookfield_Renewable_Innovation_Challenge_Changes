# Welcome to the 2027 Brookfield Renewable Innovation Challenge!  
As global temperatures rise, biodiversity declines, and natural resources become increasingly limited, the need for effective climate mitigation, climate adaptation, and ecosystem protection strategies has never been more urgent. Institutions like the University of Waterloo have a critical role to play in demonstrating innovative, sustainable solutions that reduce environmental impact, strengthen resilience to climate change, and safeguard the natural systems that support healthy communities and economies. Through leadership in research, operations, education, and community engagement, universities can help accelerate the transition to a low-carbon future while protecting and restoring ecosystems that provide essential services such as carbon sequestration, water filtration, flood mitigation, and biodiversity conservation. 

Brookfield Renewable operates one of the world’s largest publicly traded platforms for renewable power and decarbonization solutions. Their diversified portfolio spans hydroelectric, wind, solar, distributed energy, and sustainable technologies across five continents, driving progress toward a low-carbon future. As a global leader in clean energy innovation, Brookfield Renewable reports that it actively supports initiatives that accelerate the transition to resilient, sustainable communities. 

## Challenge Description 

The University of Waterloo is seeking  transformative, campus-wide solutions that achieve deep reductions in greenhouse gas emissions, protect local ecosystems, and enhance resilience to climate change impacts such as extreme heat, storms, flooding, and power outages. The University is focussing on three sustainability goals: thriving ecosystems, climate action, and climate resilience. 

Your team is tasked with developing actionable strategies that would progress one of the three campus sustainability goals within UW’s campus infrastructure: buildings, energy systems, transportation systems, and water systems. You will be creating a solution that can be deployed on campus in 3-5 years, with positive impacts until 2050. While there are no budget limitations for your proposed projects, proposed solutions should consider a reasonable balance between resources spent, technical feasibility and the project’s long-term contribution to decarbonizing campus.  

Proposed solutions should consider technical and economic feasibility, community acceptance, and long-term sustainability, integrating innovative ideas that align with society’s vision for a sustainable future. Your team must hit the ground running — analysing data, exploring campus sites, and refining your ideas —  to deliver a compelling, well-supported solution by the end of the weekend!

## Campus Sustainability Goals 
Your team will need to select one (or more) goals to pursue. 
### Climate Action 
Climate action involves reducing greenhouse gas emissions and supporting the transition to a low-carbon future. In Canada, this includes improving energy efficiency, adopting renewable energy, electrifying vehicle fleets, expanding public transit, reducing waste, purchasing sustainable products, and supporting green building design. These actions help address challenges such as wildfires, floods, extreme weather, and rising temperatures while contributing to a more sustainable and resilient future.

### Climate Resilience 
Climate resilience is the ability of communities, infrastructure, and natural systems to prepare for, adapt to, and recover from climate-related impacts. Canadian resilience efforts include flood mitigation, climate-smart infrastructure, emergency preparedness, urban tree planting, stormwater management improvements, drought planning, and incorporating climate risks into long-term decision-making. These measures help protect public safety, reduce disruptions, and support community well-being in a changing climate.

### Thriving Ecosystems 
Thriving ecosystems are healthy, diverse natural environments that support biodiversity, clean water, clean air, and climate regulation. In Canada, protecting and restoring forests, wetlands, grasslands, rivers, pollinator habitats, and coastal areas helps strengthen ecological health and climate resilience. Activities such as habitat restoration, invasive species management, sustainable land stewardship, native species planting, and watershed protection contribute to healthy ecosystems that benefit both nature and communities.

## 🏘 Building Performance

### Challenge A
You are tasked with selecting a University of Waterloo building and proposing a retrofit strategy to significantly reduce its energy consumption and carbon footprint. Whether you’re upgrading an aging "classic" like MC or optimizing a newer facility like E7 for extreme weather, your solution must maintain occupant comfort and operational functionality. Beyond just saving energy, your proposal should consider cost-effectiveness, human accessibility, and broader campus benefits, culminating in a pitch or prototype for judging.

### Challenge B
This challenge requires you to design a new academic building at the University of Waterloo that serves as a model for low-carbon construction and climate resilience. Unlike a retrofit, you will start from the basics like site selection, material choice to reduce embodied carbon, and passive design to ensure the building can handle future weather extremes while supporting modern campus life. 

Your goal is to balance peak energy efficiency with long term adaptability, presenting your vision through architectural models, simulations, or detailed design drawings.

## ⚡ Sustainable Energy Systems

Around the world, institutions are working to transition toward more sustainable energy systems in response to the growing impacts of climate change, habitat loss, and resource depletion. As non-renewable energy sources become increasingly scarce and costly, the urgency to adopt cleaner, more reliable alternatives continues to grow. Universities, as centres of research and innovation, play a critical role in leading this transition. 


### Challenge

This challenge asks you to develop a strategic plan to modernize the University of Waterloo’s energy infrastructure by transitioning toward cleaner, more resilient power sources. You will analyze how campus energy demand fluctuates with the weather and propose the integration of renewable technologies, such as solar or geothermal, to replace or supplement current fossil fuel use.

Your goal is to create a feasible solution that manages peak demand and slashes emissions while fitting within the constraints of the existing campus grid, presented through simulations, models, or data-driven prototypes.

## 💧 Water Protection
Water has become a pressing problem in the last decades, with experts warning that human activities causing pollution and climate change are drastically changing the water cycles we depend on for life. Water is one of the University of Waterloo's most important resources, supporting building operations, research activities, cooling and heating infrastructure, laboratories, residence facilities, landscaping, and daily campus life. In 2025, the University used approximately 648,936 m³ of water, representing a significant demand on regional water resources. Moreover, the Region of Waterloo is currently facing a water capacity crisis which led to the halting of new development in the region in 2026.  We need to find ways to make water consumption more sustainable, to ensure food and water security and the continuation of life.

### Challenge
Your challenge is to propose a solution to improve the sustainability, resilience, or efficiency of water systems at the University of Waterloo.  Teams may focus on water conservation, stormwater management, water reuse, water quality protection, reducing the impacts of salt use, or improving water monitoring and infrastructure management. Solutions should be technically feasible, economically viable, and capable of delivering measurable environmental benefits.

For more information and a step-by-step guide on how to implement various tools into your solution, check out the [Water_Protection Folder](./Water_Protection).

## Presentation Format
Final presentations to judges will be 5 minutes long with an additional 5 minutes for questions from the judges.   

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) are a global framework of 17 interconnected goals adopted in 2015 to address social, economic, and environmental challenges by 2030. They promote poverty reduction, quality education, gender equality, clean energy, sustainable communities, responsible consumption, climate action, and ecosystem protection. In Canada, the SDGs help guide governments, businesses, Indigenous communities, institutions, and non-profit organizations in advancing sustainability while improving quality of life. The goals recognize that economic prosperity, social inclusion, and environmental stewardship are linked, encouraging collaborative action to create a more equitable, resilient, and sustainable future for current and future generations. 

Your presentation must include:  
- the SDGs that are relevant to your project. 
- The problem you are tackling and the specific site(s) on campus  
- An overview of your solution(s). Include pictures/screen captures of any prototypes, models, or drawings of your solution 
- A discussion on the impact of the proposed solution on the University of Waterloo’s goals 
- An overview of the economics of the project 
- Disclosure of how genAI tools were used for any task during the Challenge. 
- Make sure you pay attention to the judging criteria when you are putting your presentation together! 

From each group, please submit the group number, solution title and description, and your presentation slides using [this form](https://forms.office.com/r/xAKjnD8DTv).
